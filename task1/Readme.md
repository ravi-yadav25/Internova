## 1. Introduction to Cyber Security

### What is Cyber Security?

We live in a world surrounded by technology, and we use it in almost every task we perform. We share and store personal and sensitive information across websites, applications, and digital platforms. Protecting this information is essential, and this is where cybersecurity plays an important role.

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, mobile devices, networks, applications, and data against unauthorized access, theft, damage, disruption, and misuse. It combines technology, security policies, and user awareness to protect digital systems and information from cyber threats.

### Importance of Cyber Security

Cybersecurity is important because individuals, businesses, hospitals, banks, and government organizations depend on computers and digital systems in their daily operations.

It helps to:

- Protect sensitive personal, financial, and business information.
- Prevent unauthorized access and cyberattacks.
- Build trust among customers, employees, and business partners.
- Protect online transactions and digital services.
- Reduce the risk of data theft, financial loss, and reputational damage.
- Maintain the availability and reliability of digital systems.

A cyberattack can expose personal information, cause financial theft, interrupt services, lead to blackmail, damage an organization's reputation, and even threaten national security.

### Main Objectives of Cyber Security

The main objectives of cybersecurity are:

1. **Confidentiality:** Ensuring that information is accessible only to authorized users.
2. **Integrity:** Maintaining the accuracy and completeness of information.
3. **Availability:** Ensuring that systems and information remain accessible when required.

These three objectives are commonly known as the CIA Triad.

---

## 2. CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is a fundamental model of information security. It consists of three important principles:

### 1. Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that only authorized individuals can access sensitive information.

**Example:** Passwords, encryption, and access controls protect a patient's medical records from unauthorized access.

### 2. Integrity

Integrity ensures that data remains accurate, complete, and unchanged unless modified by an authorized user.

**Example:** Digital signatures and checksums help prevent unauthorized modification of bank transaction records.

### 3. Availability

Availability ensures that authorized users can access systems and data whenever they need them.

**Example:** Backups, redundant servers, and DDoS protection help keep online banking services available.

## 4. Common Cyber Threats

Cyber threats are malicious activities that can damage systems, steal information, or interrupt digital services. The following are five common cyber threats.

### 4.1 Malware

**Definition:**  
Malware is malicious software designed to gain unauthorized access to a system, steal data, or damage networks and devices.

**How It Works:**

Malware typically enters a system through infected email attachments, malicious downloads, or software vulnerabilities. Once installed, it may steal data, corrupt files, or provide attackers with remote control over the infected system.

**Prevention Methods:**

- Keep operating systems, browsers, and applications updated.
- Use automatically updated antivirus or anti-malware software.
- Download applications only from trusted sources.
- Avoid unknown email attachments and suspicious links.
- Use email and web filters to block malicious domains and downloads.

### 4.2 Virus

**Definition:**  
A computer virus is a type of malware that attaches itself to a file or program and spreads when the infected file or program is executed.

**How It Works:**

When a user opens an infected document or executable file, the virus activates and may replicate itself into other files or programs. This can corrupt data, slow down the computer, or spread to other devices.

**Prevention Methods:**

- Scan USB drives, downloads, and email attachments before opening them.
- Enable real-time antivirus protection.
- Avoid unexpected or suspicious attachments.
- Maintain offline or cloud backups to restore infected files safely.

### 4.3 Ransomware

**Definition:**  
Ransomware is malicious software that encrypts files or locks users out of their computers and demands a ransom, usually in cryptocurrency, to restore access.

**How It Works:**

Ransomware is commonly delivered through phishing emails, malicious downloads, or exploitation of unpatched systems. Once installed, it encrypts files and displays a ransom demand. Some variants may also steal data and threaten to publish it.

**Prevention Methods:**

- Maintain regular and tested backups in an offline or separate secure location.
- Keep operating systems and software updated.
- Train users to identify phishing emails and suspicious links.
- Restrict remote access services from being directly exposed to the internet.
- Use multi-factor authentication.

### 4.4 Phishing

**Definition:**  
Phishing is a social engineering attack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick users into revealing passwords, payment details, or other sensitive information.

**How It Works:**

Attackers send fake emails, text messages, calls, or websites that appear genuine. For example, they may create a fake bank alert or delivery notification. When a victim clicks a malicious link or enters information on a fake website, the attacker captures the information or may install malware.

**Prevention Methods:**

- Verify sender addresses, links, and requests before responding.
- Never enter passwords or financial information through unexpected message links.
- Use anti-phishing browser protection.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Conduct regular security awareness checks.

### 4.5 Denial-of-Service (DoS) Attack

**Definition:**  
A Denial-of-Service (DoS) attack attempts to make a server, website, or network unavailable by overwhelming it with excessive traffic or requests.

**How It Works:**

The attacker sends more connection requests, packets, or queries than the target system can process. As a result, legitimate users may be unable to access the service.

**Prevention Methods:**

- Use firewalls and intrusion detection systems.
- Monitor network traffic to identify unusual activity.
- Configure rate limiting.
- Use load balancing and redundant infrastructure.
- Monitor systems for abnormal traffic patterns.

## 6. Conclusion

Cybersecurity is an essential part of the modern digital world. It protects computers, networks, applications, and sensitive information from unauthorized access and malicious activities.

The CIA Triad — Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability — provides the foundation for understanding information security. Learning about threats such as malware, viruses, ransomware, phishing, and DoS attacks helps users understand common risks and the importance of preventive security practices.

By following basic cybersecurity measures, individuals and organizations can improve their protection and build a safer digital environment.
